# Songbird: product

## One line

**Songbird is a music journal. Every day you live becomes a single.**

Drop in a few photos from today. Songbird hears what kind of day it was, calls its genre, turns the things in your
photos into the band, records the song, and cuts the music video. About three minutes, and it is yours to keep and to send.

Hook: *every day has a genre.* Sign-off: *your days, on record.*

## The problem (impact)

- People take more than two trillion photos a year (2025 estimate, PetaPixel) and look back at almost none of them.
  A camera roll keeps what a day looked like. It is bad at keeping how the day felt.
- Songs are the memory format humans never lose. In a 2016 study, memories cued by music were rated more vivid than
  memories cued by famous faces (Belfi, Karlan & Tranel, *Memory* 24(7)). People still know every word of a song from
  when they were fifteen.
- Almost nobody can write, sing, produce and edit a song about their own Tuesday. Songbird gives everyone with a phone
  a producer, a band and a video crew, built on open-weights music and Meta's models.

So the product is not a novelty song generator. It turns the camera roll, the most-used and least-revisited archive
people own, into something they will replay, share and remember: one single per day, a discography per year.

## Who it is for

Anyone with a camera roll and a day worth keeping, which is every day. The shelf proves the range: a first day at a
new job, moving day, a first half marathon, a night shift, a rainy Sunday of nothing, a friend's wedding, a pitch that
went wrong, a first solo trip, a seventy-fourth birthday at the allotment. Not a kids' product, not a party trick.

## What makes it Songbird

1. **Your photos are the band.** SAM 3.1 cuts each object out, Muse Image dresses it as its band role. Each thing
   gets a part and a costume. The photos are not decoration; they are the cast.
2. **The genre is a verdict.** The producer listens to the day and commits: "1983 italo disco", "1930s golden-age
   tango", with a dry, specific reason.
3. **It is a real record.** Four takes, a tuner that cuts the off-key ones, an A&R ear that keeps one, lyrics sung
   and checked by Voice Transcribe, a sleeve, a video directed shot by shot.
4. **It is a journal.** Releases are dated days. Every release gets a catalogue number (SB-0021). Every single you make
   is kept on your device and shown on the front door as "your discography", so over time you get a record of your
   own life. No account needed.

## Voice

Lowercase, warm, dry, sure of itself. Talks about days and songs, never about "AI magic". The producer has opinions.
No emoji, no "AI-powered", no exclamation marks. Short sentences.

## Truth rules

- Every release on the shelf was made by the real pipeline. The sample days' photos are generated by Muse Image so
  no real person's photos are used, and the page says so.
- Numbers on cards (lines sung, in-key %, takes) come from the tape, never invented.
