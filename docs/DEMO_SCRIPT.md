# Demo voiceover

About 2 minutes of speech in a 2:55 video. The music carries the rest: the cold open, and half a minute of the
finished single.

## How to record

- One file, on your phone's voice memo app or any mic. A quiet room with soft things around (a closet full of
  clothes is perfect). Hold the phone a hand's width from your mouth, slightly to the side.
- Read each numbered part, then **pause for two full seconds** before the next one. I split the file on those pauses
  and lay each part over its moment, so the cut fits your pace, not the other way round.
- Talk like you're showing a friend, not presenting. Slightly slower than feels natural. If you fumble a line, pause
  and just say that part again; I'll use the last take.
- Drop the file in `film/demo/` (any format: m4a, mp3, wav).

## The script

**1 · the problem** *(over the front door's sky)*
> We take two trillion photos a year, and we almost never look at them again.
> A camera roll remembers what a day looked like. It forgets how it felt.
> But you still know every word of a song you loved at fifteen.

**2 · the product**
> So I built Songbird. A music journal, where every day you live becomes a single.

**3 · the day** *(the desk)* ✱
> This is Maya's day. First day at a new job. Five photos, and one line: "Didn't get lost once. OK, once."
> Let's make it a song.

**4 · the room, first half** *(look, cut, produce)*
> This is the control room. Every lamp is a Meta model, and it only lights while it's actually working.
> Muse Spark looks at the whole day. SAM cuts out the things in each photo; they're the band.
> Then Muse Wonder, the producer, calls the genre, with a reason, and writes a line about every photo.

**5 · the room, second half** *(record, tune, judge)*
> While the band records, Muse Image paints the sleeve and dresses everything for its part, and Spark writes the
> video's shot list. Four takes come back. Meta's Demucs pulls the singer apart from the band, so we can measure the
> key, and any take that's off gets cut. Spark 1.2 listens to the rest and keeps the best one. Voice Transcribe checks
> every line was really sung, and MMS times every word.

**6 · the result** ✱ *(then the song plays)*
> Under four minutes later, Maya has a single.

**7 · how it works**
> Under the hood, that's eleven jobs. Ten of them are done by eight Meta models, and three run on open weights, on my
> own GPU. The hard part wasn't making a song. It was teaching Songbird to throw out its own bad takes. Demucs caught our singer
> in a different key from the band. MMS moved the lyrics from drifting to landing on the beat. Every one of those
> decisions is in the log.

**8 · every day**
> And every day has a genre. A night shift at the bakery is eurodance. A first half marathon is heavy metal.
> Save it as a vertical video for the group chat, and it stays in your discography. One day, one song.

**9 · the close**
> Songbird. Your days, on record. It's live right now: make one, or watch one get made.
